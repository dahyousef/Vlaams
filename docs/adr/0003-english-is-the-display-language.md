# English is the display language; the learner's native language is a different fact

English is the default display language and French a toggle. The language a learner *reads*
is a setting. The language they *already speak* is not a setting — it is a fact about them
that decides which Dutch words come free.

The app is public and gets handed to colleagues, and the learner prefers reading English.
The distinction matters because some reference material is anchored to French rather than
written in it: the pronunciation page explains Dutch vowels through French ones, and two
further pages cover French words Flemish kept and French false friends. Those stay in French
whatever the display language is, because translating them destroys what they are for.

## Considered options

A setting for the learner's native language was rejected. Exactly three pieces of material
depend on it, and labelling each page with the language it helps solves the problem without
asking the learner a question they would have to understand first.
