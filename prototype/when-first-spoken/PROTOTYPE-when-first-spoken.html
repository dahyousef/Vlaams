When does a session first ask you to say an item aloud?

One item climbs four ladders side by side. Each column shows the rung the item is on, the exercise it gets, the day it is next due, and above all the day it is first asked aloud.

The question. Phase 1 leaves the ladder as it is. An item is asked aloud once it stands on rung 5, and the wait before that exercise is the longest of the ladder: 21 days. So a session first asks you to say a word about five weeks after you meet it. For someone who works among Dutch speakers, is that too late?

If it is, the plan names two changes: offer say it aloud on an earlier rung (ladders B and C), or shorten the three middle waits (ladder D). Ladder A is the plan as written.

The top of this page follows one item alone: no session of 22 places, no backlog, no daily budget. What those add is in Measured on the real engine, at the bottom, and it changes the picture.

Guided walkthroughs

What happened, answer by answer

Measured on the real engine

The ladders above were also run on the phase 1 engine itself (the saved end state of the plan), with the whole course of 439 items and real sessions: 22 places, the reserved share for new items, the daily budget. Two learners, as in the plan's own simulation: one takes every session the day offers, the other one session a day. Each figure comes from one run of 200 days; a second run with a different draw agrees to within a few days.

  • Ladder A is confirmed, and with one session a day it is later than five weeks. A word met on day 1 is first asked aloud on day 35 by the learner who takes every session. With one session a day it is day 44 to 47. A word met later in the course also waits for a place in a session: the typical item is asked aloud 59 days after it is met, 74 if some answers are wrong. That is eight to ten weeks.
  • Ladders B and C are a draw, not a date. Adding say it aloud to a rung puts it beside that rung's other exercises, and the engine draws one. B reaches about a third of the items, C a bit over half. The rest are asked aloud on rung 5, exactly as on ladder A.
  • One rule the engine does not have would make them certain: an item never yet said aloud gets say it aloud as soon as its rung offers it. With it, and every answer right, C asks every item aloud about a week after it is met (every session) or two and a half weeks (one session a day); B about two weeks, or five.
  • Ladder D moves every item, but does little for one session a day. With waits of 2, 5 and 13 days the every-session learner goes from 37 days to 26. The one-session learner goes from 59 to 54, and from 74 to 71 with 85 answers right in 100. There an item is held back by the queue for a place, not by its wait.
  • What they cost. B and C serve exactly the exercises A serves when every answer is right, and take a minute or so more a day, because a spoken exercise is longer than a written one. D asks no more of an item before it is retired, eight exercises on either ladder, but brings them forward: 14 more in every 100 fall inside the first 200 days for the every-session learner, and the backlog peaks higher.
  • Grammar rules are already a draw today. Their rung 4 offers say it aloud beside “write your own”. About half of the nine rules are first asked aloud around day 13, the others around day 35. The plan's table gives day 14: that is the early half.
  • Said in the second week. Learning a word first and saying it in its second week needs three things: say it aloud on rung 4, the said-aloud-first rule, and middle waits of 2 and 4 days where the plan has 3 and 8. A word met on day 1 is then said on day 9 to 13 with every session, day 12 to 14 with one session a day. With one session a day the words met later still queue: the typical one waits 33 to 46 days. With the rule, on any ladder, spoken exercises become about a quarter of all exercises, where the plan has 15 in 100.
  • Seen along the way: spoken exercises arrive in blocks. Rung 5 offers nothing but say it aloud, on every ladder, and items met together arrive there together. The every-session learner meets the whole course in 13 days, and around day 38 gets a day of about a hundred spoken exercises and nothing else (107 on ladder A, 46 minutes). None of these ladders removes that.

Days are counted from the day an item is met, which is day 1. A single figure is the typical (median) item, and its bracket holds eight items in ten. Where a ladder splits the items in two, each group is given with its own typical day. Words and phrases only, 430 items; the course has no chunk today.

Each cell: every answer right / 85 right in 100. Backlog: items due at the start of a day, at its highest. Ladder D was measured with waits of 2, 5 and 13 days, and the last row with 2, 4 and 21; other values typed in its column above are not measured.

The simulated learner starts at exactly the same time every day, which is the unkind case: an item is due 24 hours after its answer, a few minutes after the next day's session began, so each wait runs a day long. Starting fifteen minutes later each day brings ladder A's day-1 word from day 44–47 to day 38–40 with one session a day, and from day 35–38 to day 34–35 with every session.

What the top of this page leaves out

  • Whole days. In the engine an item is due at the time of day it was last answered. Study earlier in the day than last time and it comes a day later. That is why the engine gives day 35 where the ladder alone says day 34.
  • Other items. A session avoids the two kinds of exercise it has just served, which nudges the draw. And a due item can wait for a place. Both are in the measured figures, not in the columns.
  • Cognates. A word that looks like its French word skips rung 0 and runs about two days ahead.
  • Speaking outside sessions. The conversations (Parler) and Écouter → Répéter après use speech from day one, on any ladder.
